# 100-Point Local SEO & Google Business Profile Audit Checklist

Use **Pass / Needs Improvement / Fail / N/A** for every applicable check.

> Local search is not controlled by a single optimization. Evaluate relevance, distance and prominence together with profile quality, website signals, reviews, citations and real-world business accuracy.

---

# A. Google Business Profile — 35 Checks

## 1. Ownership, Eligibility & Verification
- [ ] **1.** The business is eligible for a Google Business Profile under current Google guidelines.
- [ ] **2.** The correct business owner or authorized organization controls the profile.
- [ ] **3.** The profile is verified and has no unresolved verification requirement.
- [ ] **4.** Owners and managers are reviewed and former employees/agencies no longer needing access are removed.
- [ ] **5.** The profile has no active suspension, disablement or major policy warning requiring action.

## 2. Business Identity
- [ ] **6.** Business name matches the real-world business name and is not stuffed with unnecessary keywords.
- [ ] **7.** Business name is consistent with prominent real-world branding and the website.
- [ ] **8.** Address is accurate and displayed only when the location legitimately serves customers there.
- [ ] **9.** Service-area businesses hide the address when required and define service areas accurately.
- [ ] **10.** Phone number is current, answered by the business and appropriate for the location.

## 3. Categories
- [ ] **11.** The primary category accurately represents the core business.
- [ ] **12.** The primary category has been compared with relevant local competitors and available category options.
- [ ] **13.** Useful secondary categories are added where they represent genuine services.
- [ ] **14.** Irrelevant categories are not being used simply to target additional keywords.
- [ ] **15.** Category choices are reviewed periodically because available categories and business offerings can change.

## 4. Core Profile Information
- [ ] **16.** Regular business hours are accurate.
- [ ] **17.** Special/holiday hours are maintained when applicable.
- [ ] **18.** Website URL points to the most appropriate, working page for the location.
- [ ] **19.** Appointment, booking, menu, order or other relevant action links are configured where supported.
- [ ] **20.** Business description clearly explains the business, services and local context without promotional spam.

## 5. Services, Products & Attributes
- [ ] **21.** Relevant services are listed accurately in the profile.
- [ ] **22.** Service descriptions and prices are maintained where useful and supported.
- [ ] **23.** Products/menu information is maintained where appropriate for the business type.
- [ ] **24.** Applicable business attributes are completed accurately.
- [ ] **25.** Profile information does not contradict the website, storefront or actual customer experience.

## 6. Photos, Video & Visual Trust
- [ ] **26.** Logo and cover imagery are current and professionally representative.
- [ ] **27.** The profile contains authentic exterior/interior/location imagery where relevant.
- [ ] **28.** Real photos of products, services, team or customer experience are added where appropriate.
- [ ] **29.** Misleading, obsolete or low-quality owner-uploaded imagery is reviewed and improved.
- [ ] **30.** User-uploaded photos are periodically reviewed for inaccurate or inappropriate content that may need reporting.

## 7. Activity, Q&A & Multi-Location Governance
- [ ] **31.** Relevant GBP updates/posts or other supported profile content are used when they provide genuine customer value.
- [ ] **32.** Customer questions and answers are monitored for accuracy where the feature is available.
- [ ] **33.** Profile edits suggested by users/Google are monitored so important details do not silently become incorrect.
- [ ] **34.** Multi-location businesses maintain unique, accurate data for each legitimate location rather than cloning inaccurate information.
- [ ] **35.** A recurring GBP maintenance process exists for hours, links, photos, services, reviews, access and policy changes.

# B. Website & Local Landing Pages — 25 Checks

## 8. Location Relevance
- [ ] **36.** The website clearly states what the business does and where it operates.
- [ ] **37.** The GBP website link resolves to a relevant indexable page rather than an unrelated generic page.
- [ ] **38.** Primary services have useful, substantive website content.
- [ ] **39.** Local terminology is used naturally where it helps users understand the service area.
- [ ] **40.** Important location information is visible to users and not hidden only inside images or scripts.

## 9. Location Pages
- [ ] **41.** Each genuine physical location has a dedicated page when appropriate.
- [ ] **42.** Location pages contain unique information rather than near-identical city-name substitutions.
- [ ] **43.** Each location page displays accurate name, address and phone details where applicable.
- [ ] **44.** Location-specific hours are displayed when useful.
- [ ] **45.** Location pages explain services available at that specific location.

## 10. Local Proof & Helpfulness
- [ ] **46.** Location pages include authentic local proof such as team, facility, projects, customers or testimonials where appropriate.
- [ ] **47.** Directions, parking, access instructions or landmarks are included when genuinely useful to visitors.
- [ ] **48.** Embedded maps are used where helpful without replacing crawlable address information.
- [ ] **49.** Testimonials/reviews shown on the site are genuine and relevant.
- [ ] **50.** Frequently asked local questions are answered based on real customer needs rather than keyword stuffing.

## 11. On-Page Local SEO
- [ ] **51.** Page titles accurately combine service/business intent with location where appropriate.
- [ ] **52.** H1 and primary page copy clearly communicate the page's purpose.
- [ ] **53.** Meta descriptions are useful and location-relevant rather than duplicated at scale.
- [ ] **54.** Images have descriptive filenames/alt text when appropriate, without geographic keyword stuffing.
- [ ] **55.** Internal links help users and crawlers reach important location and service pages.

## 12. Service-Area & Multi-Location Content
- [ ] **56.** Service-area pages exist only where the business genuinely serves the area and can provide useful unique information.
- [ ] **57.** Thin doorway-style city pages are avoided.
- [ ] **58.** Multi-location navigation makes individual locations easy to find.
- [ ] **59.** Store/location finder functionality remains crawlable and usable where implemented.
- [ ] **60.** Closed, moved or merged location pages are handled with an intentional redirect/content strategy.

# C. Reviews & Reputation — 15 Checks

## 13. Review Acquisition
- [ ] **61.** The business has an ongoing process for asking genuine customers for reviews.
- [ ] **62.** Review requests are sent at appropriate points in the customer journey.
- [ ] **63.** Customers are not offered prohibited incentives in exchange for positive Google reviews.
- [ ] **64.** Review gating practices that selectively discourage unhappy customers from reviewing publicly are avoided.
- [ ] **65.** Review links or QR codes are tested and make the process easy for customers.

## 14. Review Quality & Recency
- [ ] **66.** New reviews arrive with reasonable ongoing recency rather than only in isolated bursts.
- [ ] **67.** Review patterns appear authentic and are not dependent on purchased/fabricated reviews.
- [ ] **68.** Review feedback reflects the real products/services and customer experience.
- [ ] **69.** Review sentiment is analyzed for recurring operational strengths and weaknesses.
- [ ] **70.** Location-specific review performance is monitored separately for multi-location businesses.

## 15. Review Response & Reputation Operations
- [ ] **71.** Reviews are monitored consistently.
- [ ] **72.** Positive reviews receive appropriate, non-mechanical responses when useful.
- [ ] **73.** Negative reviews receive professional responses without exposing private customer information.
- [ ] **74.** Reviews believed to violate platform policy are documented and reported through appropriate channels rather than argued with publicly.
- [ ] **75.** Review volume, rating, recency, themes and response performance are included in ongoing local SEO reporting.

- [ ] **77.** Major search engines/maps and important local directories show accurate business data.
- [ ] **78.** High-value industry-specific directories contain accurate listings where relevant.
- [ ] **79.** High-value regional/local directories contain accurate listings where relevant.
- [ ] **80.** Important citation listings link to the correct website/location page where possible.

## 17. Citation Hygiene
- [ ] **81.** Major NAP inconsistencies have been identified and corrected.
- [ ] **82.** Duplicate listings on important platforms are identified and resolved where possible.
- [ ] **83.** Old addresses and phone numbers from previous locations are reviewed and corrected on important sources.
- [ ] **84.** Citation work prioritizes authoritative/relevant platforms rather than bulk low-quality directory submissions.
- [ ] **85.** Citation accuracy is re-audited after moves, rebrands, phone changes, mergers or new-location launches.

- [ ] **88.** Mobile experience supports calls, directions, forms and bookings without major friction.
- [ ] **89.** Core location pages load reliably and do not suffer from severe performance problems.
- [ ] **90.** HTTPS, preferred hostname and key business URLs are configured consistently.

## 19. Structured Data & Measurement
- [ ] **91.** Appropriate Organization/LocalBusiness structured data is implemented accurately where applicable.
- [ ] **92.** Structured data uses the most specific appropriate business type available without misrepresenting the business.
- [ ] **93.** Schema name, address, telephone, URL, opening hours and other properties agree with visible page content where included.
- [ ] **94.** Calls, forms, bookings and other meaningful local conversions are measured with analytics/tagging appropriate to the business.
- [ ] **95.** GBP performance and website conversion data are reviewed together instead of judging success only by rankings.

- [ ] **96.** Priority local queries are manually reviewed from relevant locations/devices to understand actual SERP composition.
- [ ] **97.** Legitimate high-performing competitors are compared for categories, reviews, website quality, local content and authority signals.
- [ ] **98.** Competitor analysis distinguishes sustainable strengths from apparent guideline violations or spam.
- [ ] **99.** Suspected fake businesses, keyword-stuffed names or other policy violations are documented carefully and reported only through legitimate channels when appropriate.
- [ ] **100.** Local SEO priorities are based on business impact and realistic competitive gaps rather than copying every competitor tactic.

## Prioritization

After scoring, label failed/weak checks:

- **Critical** — profile eligibility, suspension, wrong identity/address, inaccessible pages, serious policy or ownership problems.
- **High** — directly affects discoverability, trust, conversions or major location accuracy.
- **Medium** — meaningful improvement opportunity.
- **Low** — refinement, maintenance or lower-impact enhancement.

Do not prioritize solely by the number of failed checks. Fix foundational accuracy, policy, accessibility and conversion issues first.
